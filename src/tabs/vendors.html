What it works out

Every marketable item an NPC sells for plain gil, priced against the market board on your world. The vendor’s price never changes, so a good row is a run you can repeat rather than a one-off snipe.

Reading the table

Bought from is the NPC, zone and map spot; +n more means other vendors stock it too. locked? means a quest or achievement may gate the shop, and seasonal that it only opens during an event. Sell price on Realistic uses the lower of the cheapest listing and the 30-day average (the middle price of real sales), since you will have to undercut to sell.

Worth knowing

Only profitable items show by default, sorted by Gil/day, so steady sellers beat huge margins that never sell. Most vendor stock has barely sold, so ⚠ is common: Hide ⚠ outliers drops those rows, and Show ALL vendor items brings back the losers too.

Pricing vendor stock against the market board…

Vendor cost is the fixed gil price the NPC charges — it never changes, so the only moving part is what the board pays. Sell price on Realistic takes the lower of the cheapest current listing and the 30-day average, because you have to undercut the board to sell but a lone silly listing is not a real price. ⚠ means the price is not backed by real sales — either nothing has sold in 30 days, or the listing sits far from the average that did. Most vendor stock is dead stock, so expect to see it a lot; the headline figures above ignore those rows entirely, and Hide ⚠ outliers drops them from the table. A seasonal tag means the shop only opens during an event. Profit/unit = sell × (1 − tax) − vendor cost. Gil/day = net sale price × units sold per day — a stack of 99 counts as 99, not as one sale — so a 40M margin on something that sells twice a year sinks below a 5k margin on something that shifts 30 a day. That is the market’s pace rather than your cut; for what you actually keep, read Profit/unit against it, or the Best profit / day card. Locations come from the game's own shop data; many items are stocked by several NPCs, so +n more means you have closer options.
