How a voyage is valued. Each sector a sub surveys hands back a loot roll, and a second one when its favor clears the sector’s breakpoint. What comes back — and how many — is read from Infi’s crowd-sourced loot records (the SubmarineTracker plugin’s uploads, updated recently): units per roll × rolls per visit, averaged over every recorded voyage. A build that falls short of a sector’s surveillance breakpoints loses that tier’s loot, and short of its retrieval breakpoint brings back the smaller quantity band; both are flagged on the route. The breakpoints themselves are SubmarineTracker’s. Salvage is valued at the NPC price, which never moves; everything else at the lower of its 30-day average and its cheapest listing across your data centre, after tax, so a single troll sale cannot make a sector. Repairs are Magitek Repair Materials at the current price, spread over the voyages a part lasts. Ceruleum tanks cost company credits rather than gil, so they are counted but not subtracted. Voyage time is the game’s own formula (travel and survey time scaled by speed, plus a fixed 12 hours), and a route’s sectors are sailed in whichever order is quickest within your range. Profit/day is what the fleet nets a day when you resend on the schedule you picked — a 22-hour voyage sent once a day earns a day’s worth, a 26-hour one only every second day. On the Loot view, Gil/day = Avg 30d × (1 − tax) × units/day, the market’s pace across your data centre, as everywhere else on the desk. Data via Universalis.
